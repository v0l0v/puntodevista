‘Remember Me’ Highlights Black Americans’ Self-Expression in Early Photography

In our era of screens and proliferous imagery, it strains the imagination to comprehend a time before photography. Two centuries ago, an inventor named Nicéphore Niépce took the very first picture of his courtyard in France by fitting a pewter plate into a camera obscura. A few years later, a professional scene painter named Louis-Jacques-Mandé Daguerre conceived of the aptly named daguerreotype, in which the image could be permanently fixed. He and Niépce both sold their technologies to the French government, which then published a how-to guide and facilitated the global spread of the process. In 1939, the first daguerrotypes were created in the U.S.

A Day With Brian Hippern and the Lomo'Instant Automat Glass

Seattle-based collage artist and illustrator Brian Hippern creates vibrant, hand-cut compositions inspired by nature, found materials, and the quiet moments of everyday life. Working with colored pencil drawings, vintage paper, handwritten letters, and carefully collected ephemera, his intuitive process balances thoughtful composition with spontaneity, allowing each piece to evolve organically. For this edition of A Day With, we asked Brian to document a weekend in his creative life, from neighborhood coffee stops and treasure hunting for collage materials to long hours at the craft table bringing new works to life. Armed with the Lomo'Instant Automat Glass, he captured the people, places, and rituals that shape his artistic practice.
