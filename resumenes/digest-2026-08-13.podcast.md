Turn the volume up! includes works by a diverse group of contemporary artists and photographers, from Spider Martin ’s dramatic documentation of Freedom Marches during the Civil Rights Movement to Kara Walker ’s multimedia interrogations of U.S. history and racial stereotypes. Visitors will also encounter works by the Guerilla Girls, Carrie Mae Weems, Kiki Smith , Bruce Davidson, and many more. Kara Walker, Confederate Prisoners Being Conducted from Jonesborough to Atlanta from the portfolio Harper's Pictorial History of the Civil War (Annotated)' (2005), offset lithograph and screenprint on paper, edition 35/35, 39 x 53 inches. © Kara Walker, photo by Peter Paul Geoffrion The Nasher has organized the exhibition into five thematic areas: Remarks, Organize, Records, Dissents, and Petitions. Photographs, sculptures, installations, paintings, and more illustrate the array of visual languages artists use to convey important messages, influencing how people discuss issues and are spurred toward civic action. The Guerilla Girls, for example, famously pasted posters around Manhattan in the 1980s that shone a light on the glaring lack of representation of women artists in not only visual art but across popular culture. Davidson is renowned for illuminating social inequality in visceral photographs, from the Jim Crow South to impoverished neighborhoods in New York City. And Carol Summers' bold screenprint of a giant red X over a photo of a Vietnamese mother and her children was part of a campaign by Artists and Writers Protest urging the end of the war in Vietnam. Throughout the show's run, pieces will be rotated in and out so that visitors can experience a wide array of works in the museum's collection. Turn the volume up! opens on August 27 and will continue through July 11, 2027. Everything Now All at Once also continues through November 1. **Tiny Questions with the Lomomatic 110: Aimee France**
Fotógrafos: chloefuller

How much can we discover about someone through a handful of tiny questions? In this series, we’re exploring that very idea. With the tiny Lomomatic 110 in hand, we’re capturing the personalities, routines, and little moments that make someone who they are. Not long backstories, but honest answers, everyday rituals, and the unexpected details that reveal a life. This time, we sat down with Aimee France for a very Aimee edition of Tiny Questions. Whether it's a three-drink minimum at breakfast or baking cakes that look like they've come straight out of a storybook, Aimee is known for her love of food, baking, and finding beauty in the quirky details of the everyday. We get into the tiny questions that make you pause and romanticize summer, from the rituals and routines to the little moments that make the season feel special. From berry-picking plans to beach days and nostalgic summer soundtracks, these Tiny Questions offer a glimpse into the world through Aimee's eyes. Photo by Aimee France ,  Shot with the Lomomatic 110 on Color Tiger 110 Have you shot film before? No. Only if my friends asks me to take photos on their cameras, and I need heavy instruction. What were your first impressions of shooting 110 film? I was confused by the setup initially. I needed a manual or a YouTube video. It's such a unique camera! Like how does the film even fit in the camera? My first photo was in front of Carmelo's, and I had to take it like seven times. It was so funny. What surprised you about shooting with the Lomomatic 110? How people thought it was so cool that I was using a film camera. Photo by Aimee France ,  Shot with the Lomomatic 110 on Color Tiger 110 Favorite thing about shooting with film for the past few weeks? I take so many pictures every day on my phone, but there's something so special about taking out a camera. You know how people say “stop and smell the roses?” It's like that. You slow down for a second and live in the moment. You actually remember the photo you took. We take so many pictures on our phones. Looking through the film was so fun because I was like, “Oh my God, I actually remember trying to take that!” Most “Aimee” photo you got back? Maybe the one on the table with the two drinks, or the beach one. I really like that beach one. Photo by Aimee France ,  Shot with the Lomomatic 110 on Color Tiger 110 Favorite film? LomoChrome Purple! I was telling all my friends it looked like I had a 2014 filter on my photos. One place you want to bring the Lomomatic next? I think when I go visit my parents. Just New England in the summer. I want to document the berry picking. Photo by Aimee France ,  Shot with the Lomomatic 110 on Color Tiger 110 What is the Aimee official coffee drink of the summer? In the morning, a hot Americano. I need hot coffee no matter what. Later in the day, a coconut water coffee slushie! Frozen coconut water with chilled espresso. Current three-drink minimum lineup? Hot Americano with honey, green juice, kefir watered down with frozen blueberries and bee pollen, and hot citrus chia seed water with yuzu juice and Celtic salt. Kind of Nature's Gatorade. Why do you think breakfast requires a multi-drink balance? I think I wake up dehydrated, and I like a lot of different flavors. The way people pair alcohol with food, I like pairing drinks with breakfast. Photo by Aimee France ,  Shot with the Lomomatic 110 on Color Tiger 110 What's one thing you're saying yes to this summer? Any plans. If someone invites me to something, there's a 98% chance I'll go. I'm in it for the bit. I had an epiphany this summer that I haven't really lived life in the past five years. I used to have a business making cakes, and when you're a self-employed baker, your life revolves around the process. It feels amazing to have a social life again and have the energy for new connections. What's one thing you're trying to get better at this summer? Working on my social skills. **Gold-tinged togetherness as Londoners flock to Primrose Hill for the solar eclipse**

Celestial gathering — Ella Glossop photographs the once in decades event from one the city’s most popular gathering spots, and the community spirit that encapsulated the moment. London hasn’t seen a solar eclipse this big since 1999.Now in the big year or 2026, as thousands flock to the peak of Primrose Hill for the spectacle, there’s still something ’90s about it. Maybe it’s DIY-ness of all the tech: Meta glasses aren’t going to cut it here, so it’s holographic plastic lenses on cardboard straps. Phone cameras aren’t any good at picturing the sun, so people have brought different camera set ups, colanders, cereal-box projectors. Maybe it’s the fact that - during the day in summer - it’s not 35 degrees. Maybe, as the sun sets behind the moon and the light flattens, it’s the weird feeling that things might be okay.A two-hour long affair – the eclipse reached its peak at 7:13PM, where over 90% of the sun is covered. We won’t see coverage this full again until 2081. The moment draws a big cheer from the crowd – who are huddled shoulder-to-shoulder on picnic rugs, with bottles of fizz popping and boom boxes pumping tunes. Then, as the sun emerges from behind the moon and normal daylight resumes, it’s hazy like it as at 5am.Read next: Danny León pulls huge aerial in line with the solar eclipseThe modern world is fraught, big tech is big, the internet is loud – and once every few decades, we get a cosmic-sanctioned dose of perspective.Huck’s Ella Glossop was there to capture the scenes.
