### Lomography Magazine

**First Impressions: Cassie McCoy's Backyard Pool Day with the Half-frame Simple Use Reloadable Film Camera**
Fotógrafos: churbumgirl, samrubin, chloefuller

Summer has a way of making even the smallest moments feel worth remembering: backyard pool parties, melting popsicles, sun-soaked afternoons, and friends who somehow turn every gathering into a photoshoot. Lucky for us, photographer Cassie McCoy ([](https://www.instagram.com/churbumgirl/)@churbumgirl) had the new Half-frame Simple Use Reloadable Film Camera Classicolor for exactly that kind of day. Known for her emotionally rich portraits and playful approach to analogue photography, Cassie's latest series leans into the carefree side of summer without losing the thoughtful storytelling that defines her work. We caught up with her to chat about finding joy in simplicity, embracing the unexpected magic of half-frame photography, and why sometimes the best images happen when you stop chasing perfection. Photos by Cassie McCoy ,  Shot with the Half-frame Simple Use Reloadable Film Camera Classicolor Hey, Cassie, welcome back to Lomography Magazine! It's been a while since we last caught up. For readers who may not be familiar with your work, can you tell us how you first got into analogue photography, and what makes the medium so important to your creative practice? Always a pleasure to be back! I got my first point-and-shoot film camera, which was partly broken, when I was 18 at a thrift store in rural Ohio for six bucks. My introduction to film set the course for a scrappy style that eventually solidified into intense storytelling through portraits. My process will always retain its frugal roots, thanks to my dad, who was raised in a working-class mill town in southern Ohio. Work with what you have is my mantra at this point, which is also a great principle to follow when you're starting out in any artistic medium. I stay loyal to film, of course, for its look, but also because the process forces you to be in your body. Whether I'm taking fun, spontaneous shots on the street or working one-on-one with a model, carefully considering how the light hits their face to tell the vision we've built together, I'm never thinking about what's behind me or the endless scroll of my to-do list. I hear my breath. I feel the buttons beneath my fingers. I hear the shutter and feel it shake through me. I'm not scared. My eyes go wide, and I take everything in. Did you have previous experience with the half-frame format? What was your first impression of the Half-Frame Simple Use Reloadable Camera? Yes! I actually used my LC-Wide's half-frame setting for some self-portraits during the winter. My childhood home's basement was being demolished and renovated, and I thought it would be cool to shoot photos within all the mess and rebuilding. It was interesting to see how the LC-Wide's half-frame images blurred together, but I ended up really liking the stark dividing line of the Half-Frame Simple Use Camera even more. It makes me want to scribble all over the frames with my writing and see the photos laid out in a book or zine. Photos by Cassie McCoy ,  Shot with the Half-frame Simple Use Reloadable Film Camera Classicolor Tell us about your experience with the camera. Where did you go and what did you shoot? We're sad we didn't get the invite to the Midwest pool party! You're always welcome to a backyard bash in the Midwest! I went up to my friend Sarah's place in Cleveland with my best friend, Lex. Sarah and Lex have known each other for years and know how to turn even a cool-down into a catwalk. We soaked up the sun while Sarah set up her shark pool, played mermaids, got sprayed by the hose, and enjoyed popsicles and cherries, naturally. Your work often explores themes of femininity, identity, and perception, but this roll feels especially playful and carefree. What inspired you to take the camera in that direction? My recent work with portraits has taken a more serious, emotionally heavy direction that has been centered around me and my growing pains. I live a life full of constant change fueled by a drive to evolve. My friend recently compared me to Darwin’s Galapagos finches, if that helps to paint a picture. As I've grown as an artist and now being a 23-year-old woman, I’ve realized that my need to play and let loose with others is something I never want to say goodbye to; my style was built by taking my cameras to parties and concerts in college with my girls, and it's a nice feeling to know that I can tap back into that joyous yet impish energy whenever I want/need to. I needed a day to let my inner girl run around in the summer sun, splash with her friends, and to soak in the sweetness the unconditional love of creativity and connection gives me. Photos by Cassie McCoy ,  Shot with the Half-frame Simple Use Reloadable Film Camera Classicolor The Half-Frame Simple Use Camera is a reloadable camera with built-in flash and interchangeable color gel filters, and true to its name, simple to use. Which of its features did you enjoy the most, or pushed you creatively into different directions? You already know I was all over the built-in flash. Flash is an essential part of my process, no matter the time of day or location. Daylight flash can elevate a scene like nothing else. In addition to my addiction to brightness, I always get some great reactions from friends and strangers because my flash is always on. Is there a specific photo from this roll that stood out to you or left a lasting impression? What's the story behind it? The photo of my best friend Lex in her sunglasses next to the photo of the bright blue sky resonated with me so much. She's been through so much with me, not only as a friend but as a creative. Our very first hangout was actually a photoshoot I did of her! This photo feels emotional because of our connection, but also because of its freshness. It's just so simple and real. It reflects the direction my style started taking this past winter, especially when I began using borders to give my work a little room to breathe. Photo by Cassie McCoy ,  Shot with the Half-frame Simple Use Reloadable Film Camera Classicolor Around that time, I started making mock-up zines using Lomography Color Negative '92 Sun-Kissed alongside messy typography pulled from my journals, inspired by David Carson, a personal hero of mine. Those photo spreads finally combined elements I'd been wanting to bring together for years, and they also helped me process a difficult period in my life. They were never meant for anyone else, just for me to process and to express gratitude for the things in my life that kept me stable. Photo by Cassie McCoy ,  Shot with the Half-frame Simple Use Reloadable Film Camera Classicolor Was there a song playing in your head while shooting the Half-Frame Simple Use Camera? If you could describe your experience with the camera through a song, what song would that be? Yes! Angel in L.A. by Kelli Ali, the voice of the cult-classic triphop group Sneaker Pimps. Trip hop captures so many different essences at once: unconventionality, sensuality, seriousness, boldness, and playfulness. Those are feelings I hope come through in any medium I work in. Angel in L.A. is equipped with a multitude of instruments in its production that strays away from structure, yet the unexpected turns keep you engaged and excited. Kelli's fresh, flirty presence could make anyone starstruck, inviting them to have a little fun and to enjoy the dance of life. We leaned into the playful, brighter side of triphop at our pool party, still packed with the vividness that life and art has to offer. Your work often embraces imperfection, whether through experimental film stocks, unexpected color shifts, or fleeting moments. Did the simplicity of this camera encourage you to let go of control and trust the process in a different way? Absolutely. Anything I can do to simplify my process, and my life, is something I welcome now. Film can become an overcomplicated medium where people worry about every frame and chase perfection, but that should never be your first thought. In many ways, that mirrors the feminine experience that shows up in my process: learning to move beyond those expectations of perfection, whether they're from manmade molds or marked by our mothers. Photos by Cassie McCoy ,  Shot with the Half-frame Simple Use Reloadable Film Camera Classicolor Any tips for shooting the Half-Frame Simple Use Camera? Invite variety! My favorite half-frame photos come from unexpected juxtapositions. Change your subject every frame, shoot from different angles, or mix close-ups with landscapes. The pairings often become even more interesting than the individual images. Where else would you take the camera? I'd love to see this camera in action at a rooftop DJ set just after sunset, during blue hour. The colored flash gels would make for some incredible scenes. Going back to Lisbon is always on my mind. I'd love to photograph the bustle of Feira da Ladra with my friends. The juxtapositions of all the different objects from the vendors, mixed with the constant flow of people and emotions, would be incredible. I'd also take it on a bike ride with my boyfriend, @samrubin, and try capturing motion alongside Pittsburgh's bright yellow bridges. I promise to be careful! Any parting words for our readers? Take your camera everywhere, especially if you have a point-and-shoot! You never know what you'll stumble upon or who you'll run into on a grocery trip, and it's okay if you don't get the perfect shot. I've never felt burnt out by photography because I've tried to keep that mindset. When you stay lighthearted and curious, every roll will always feel like you're just starting out, and that is the best feeling in the world.
