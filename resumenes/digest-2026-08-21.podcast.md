### Lomography Magazine

**Two Days, Two Styles with Green (midori.pix) and the Lomo MC-A**
Fotógrafos: nweerapat

In this feature, we catch up with portrait photographer Green, also known as midori.pix), who recently put the Lomo MC-A to the test across two portrait sessions in completely different settings. Join us as he shares his experience with the camera, his thoughts after shooting with it, and, of course, the photographs that came out of the experience. © midori.pix Hi, Green! Could you briefly introduce yourself and tell us how you got into photography? Hello everyone! I'm Green, a.k.a midori.pix, a portrait and graduation photographer. My style leans toward warm, soft, nostalgic images with the timeless feel of classic film photography. My journey into photography began when my older brother gave me a camera and a few lenses to experiment with. From there, I started exploring different genres of photography, and before I knew it, more than ten years had passed. About five years ago, I discovered the unique charm of vintage film colors and classic film stocks. I was drawn to their distinctive look and the creative challenge they offered. Since then, I've been shooting film consistently because I want every graduation portrait and profile photo I take to feel a little different, carrying the nostalgic atmosphere and timeless character of traditional film photography. © midori.pix ,  Camera: Lomo MC-A ,  Film: LomoChrome Classicolor ISO 200 ,  Model: dinipts What was your first impression of the Lomo MC-A? And after spending some time shooting with it, how would you describe its character? My first impression was that it's incredibly compact, charming, and has a classic look. It immediately gave me the feel of a point-and-shoot camera, small enough to slip into a shirt pocket and easy to carry anywhere. But once I actually started shooting with it, I realized it has a character I'd describe as “raw, yet beautifully balanced.” The colors are rich and vibrant, with subtle vignetting around the edges that gives images that unmistakable Lomography look. What really surprised me, though, was how beautifully it renders portraits in natural light. Skin tones come out soft and smooth without looking harsh at all. For such a compact camera, it also packs in some fun creative features, like double exposure, which makes it perfect for anyone who enjoys experimenting with more expressive, artistic photographs. © midori.pix ,  Camera: Lomo MC-A ,  Film: LomoChrome Classicolor ISO 200 ,  Model: dinipts Where did you take the Lomo MC-A, and what did you choose to photograph? I actually took the MC-A out for two separate portrait sessions in two completely different locations, each with its own atmosphere and a different model, so the results had a very distinct feel. The first shoot was in Talad Noi. I took my model on a relaxed street portrait walk through the neighborhood's narrow alleyways and old buildings. It was all about strolling around, camera in hand, waiting for the right moments and shooting naturally. I focused on capturing the afternoon sunlight as it spilled across weathered concrete walls and vintage cars. The camera's character complemented the location perfectly, giving the images a raw, vintage aesthetic that really enhanced the mood of the surroundings. © midori.pix ,  Camera: Lomo MC-A ,  Film: LomoChrome Classicolor ISO 200 ,  Model: dinipts The second session took place at the Chatuchak Fish Market. This time, I wanted to experiment with indoor lighting and a completely different atmosphere. I captured candid moments of another model quietly admiring the aquariums, with the neon lights and reflections from the glass tanks falling across her face. The Lomo MC-A rendered those layers of light beautifully, giving the images a soft, mysterious, and almost cinematic quality. Across both shoots, my approach was the same: keep everything spontaneous and embrace a true point-and-shoot mindset. I didn't rely on any elaborate techniques or complicated setups, just the camera, the moment, and whatever unfolded naturally in front of me. © midori.pix ,  Camera: Lomo MC-A ,  Film: Lomography Color Negative ISO 400 ,  Model: linnpingg The Lomo MC-A comes with Auto, Aperture Priority, and Manual shooting modes. Do you think having this level of control makes the camera easier to use? Absolutely. It makes shooting both easier and a lot more enjoyable. When you're out walking with a camera, the light is constantly changing. On days when I just want to raise the camera and capture the moment, I simply switch to Auto or Aperture Priority mode. I can open up the aperture for a beautifully soft background, let the camera handle the rest, and confidently catch those spontaneous, unplanned moments. “The camera's character complemented the location perfectly, giving the images a raw, vintage aesthetic that really enhanced the mood of the surroundings.” But when I step into a darker alley or want to create a dramatic backlit silhouette, I can easily switch to Manual mode and dial everything in myself. That flexibility means the MC-A can adapt to almost any shooting style or creative idea, which is something I really appreciate. © midori.pix ,  Camera: Lomo MC-A ,  Film: Lomography Color Negative ISO 400 ,  Model: linnpingg The Lomo MC-A features Autofocus, Manual Zone Focus, manual ISO settings, and easy-to-use Exposure Compensation. Do you prefer controlling everything yourself, or do you usually let the camera take over? I'd say it's more like the camera and I share the creative process, though we occasionally have our disagreements. One thing I almost always like to control myself is Exposure Compensation, because I prefer my images to be slightly underexposed. It gives them the moody, melancholic atmosphere that's become part of my style. As for the autofocus, it's genuinely smart and performs just as you'd expect from a modern point-and-shoot. That said, it does have its stubborn moments. In very low light or when the background is particularly busy, the camera sometimes seems to hesitate, almost as if it's asking itself, “Wait...what should I focus on?” The shutter locks, and no matter how many times I press it, it simply refuses to take the shot. When that happens, I just switch over to Manual Zone Focus and take control myself. Honestly, I don't mind it at all – it adds a bit of personality to the experience. Those quirky moments keep you engaged and remind you that you're interacting with a camera, not just pressing a button. © midori.pix ,  Camera: Lomo MC-A ,  Film: Lomography Color Negative ISO 400 ,  Model: linnpingg What was your reaction when you saw the results for the first time? “That's the film look!” Point-and-shoot, simply as that! The images look very lively. The lens delivers beautiful colors, retains plenty of detail, and produces images with balanced contrast and smooth tonal transitions. There is a touch of the crispness you'd expect from a compact camera, but it still feels very natural. With a maximum shutter speed of 1/500, you'll sometimes want to shoot with a wider aperture, but that's never really an issue. What impressed me most, though, was the camera's metering. Whether I was shooting into the light, using flash, or relying solely on available light, the exposure was consistently accurate. It handled backlit scenes especially well, giving me exactly the results I was hoping for. © midori.pix ,  Camera: Lomo MC-A ,  Film: Lomography Color Negative ISO 400 ,  Model: linnpingg What kinds of situations or occasions is the Lomo MC-A best suited for? I'd say it's at its best from around 4 p.m. through sunset. The light during those hours is simply beautiful, and it's hard to take a bad photo. It's also the perfect camera to keep with you as an everyday companion, whether you're wandering around on a day off, café hopping, or spending time with friends during a graduation pre-shoot. It's also a lot of fun to experiment with Double Exposure. You can pass the camera around, take turns composing and shooting, and create something together without worrying about getting everything technically perfect. The result is a collection of photographs that feel stylish, full of character, and unlike anyone else's. © midori.pix ,  Camera: Lomo MC-A ,  Film: Lomography Color Negative ISO 400 ,  Model: linnpingg If you were to take the Lomo MC-A out for another photo walk, what song would you choose as the soundtrack for the day? I'd go with Plastic Love by Mariya Takeuchi. It's a classic Japanese city pop track from the '80s with a funky groove, but there's a subtle sense of melancholy beneath it. That combination perfectly complements the Lomo MC-A's vibrant colors and vintage character. Put on that song, wander around with the camera, and keep pressing the shutter. You'll be so immersed in the experience that you'll lose track of time. Do you have any advice for people who are interested in trying the Lomo MC-A for themselves? I’d say, “Relax and just enjoy the process.” This camera wasn’t made to produce perfectly sharp images like the latest smartphones. Some photos might come out blurry, or you might accidentally create a double exposure that leaves you wondering what happened, but that’s exactly where the charm and creativity of point-and-shoot film photography come from. Allow yourself to experiment, make mistakes, and discover what the camera can do. Once you embrace that process, you’ll start to appreciate and fall in love with this unique kind of photographic experience. © midori.pix ,  Camera: Lomo MC-A ,  Film: Lomography Color Negative ISO 400 ,  Model: linnpingg For anyone who’s preparing for a graduation shoot or looking for a photographer to capture stylish film-inspired portraits with a warm and natural mood, feel free to check out my work, have a chat, ask for advice, or inquire about availability through my Instagram: midori.pix. A huge thank you to Green for sharing his experience with the Lomo MC-A with us. The photographs he created are truly stunning and showcase the camera’s unique character so beautifully. Stay tuned to see who we’ll be interviewing next!
