The Beauty of Impermanent Existence: Graeme Williams' Collection of Found Photography

Graeme Williams is a professional photographer and avid collector from South Africa whose repository of found photographs has been growing for the past 25 years. Among his collection of apartheid-era amateur photography, Graeme found a body of work that captures the spirit of the time like no other record of the past. In this interview, we speak about the unintended beauty of ‘happy accidents’ and the analogue magic carried by these anonymous photographs.

A Love Letter to Hong Kong by Night: Photographer Ber Captures the City’s Fading Glow in Dreamlike Light

> “Photography makes you more aware of the things around you, and these moments can become meaningful memories to look back on.” – Ber Through a photographer’s eyes, the city’s nightscape is never the same twice. For this feature, Lomography invited Hong Kong photographer Ber (@ber_0225) to take the Joseph Petzval 80.5 f/1.9 Focus-coupled Bokeh Control Art Lens on an after-dark adventure through the bustling streets of Temple Street, Sham Shui Po, and Wan Chai. With little need for words, Ber lets the rhythm of manual focusing lead the way. Beneath the glow of neon lights, dreamy swirls and shimmering bokeh transform familiar street scenes into fleeting, precious memories of Hong Kong at night.
