**Now & Then in London: To Be (in Frame) or Not To Be**
Fotógrafos: angelinatacinelli

We’ve been to Vienna, Paris and Milan in our Now & Then series, where we use archival film photos and give them a certain Lomographic flair. We now find ourselves in London, a city technically classified as a forest, one with the oldest subway in the world, and where over 300 languages are spoken. I took along a few photos as inspiration, my Lomomatic 110 and Color Tiger 110 ISO 200, to recreate history. I journeyed to London on the first weekend of May for the concert of none other than Miss Olivia Dean. Given that it was my first time in the city, of course, I had to make the most of it. Over the four days that we were there, I walked a total of 95,505 steps through one of the coolest cities I have ever been to. While I took along a few photos that I had hoped to perfectly recreate, nothing went according to plan. It was my first time in the city, so I was looking at everything through a fresh perspective, without knowledge of where certain sites were and how to capture them in the perfect angle. That being said, this is what Lomography is all about. We capture life around us through its chaos and perfect imperfection. So what I present to you, dear reader, are a few side-by-side comparisons and my impressions of a city that I would return to again and again. I touched down in London at around 9 in the morning on a Saturday. After setting up at a hotel near St. Pancras Train Station, we headed into the city to grab a bite at one of the most bustling spots I've experienced: Borough Market. What began as a trading hub on the south bank of the Thames in 1014 is now full of mouth-watering food options and over 100 stalls. ## Borough Market [](https://www.lomography.com/homes/angelinatacinelli/photos/29466293) Now: Borough Market ,  Shot with the Lomomatic 110 Camera&Color Tiger 110 ISO 200, May 2026 What I love about the Lomomatic 110 is that you can never say for certain what will come out in your frames. The lens is slightly askew from where the viewfinder is, which works as a built-in experimental option. The photo I took in Borough Market is one of my favorites. First off, the partially exposed sprocket and lightened portion of the frame add a certain something I could not have recreated if I wanted to. Most of the photos, if not all, I captured without thinking. I truly took the don’t think, just shoot concept to heart, which is what makes them sort of like my own personal diary. ## Leadenhall Market [](https://www.lomography.com/homes/angelinatacinelli/photos/29615140) Now: Close-Up of Leadenhall Market ,  Shot with the Lomomatic 110 Camera&Color Tiger 110 ISO 200, May 2026 Next up on our journey was Leadenhall Market, where I happened to have the best latte of my life, and of course, for Potterheads, where Chris Columbus filmed the first Harry Potter film. The red hues of the restaurants and interior of the market jumped out to me, and the Color Tiger ISO 200 film also perfectly captured it. ## Oxford Circus [](https://www.lomography.com/homes/angelinatacinelli/photos/29466300) Now: Oxford Circus ,  Shot with the Lomomatic 110 Camera&Color Tiger 110 ISO 200, May 2026 The next day, we hopped off the Tube at Oxford Circus Station, the busiest shopping street in all of Europe. The area has around 300 shops and more than 72,000 people step through every day. The “circus” layout was designed in the 19th century to best facilitate traffic movement. I found this sign right in the center, and had to time the shot perfectly because the name of the station flashed on and off. Given that I was in the shopping center of Europe, I also had to pick up a few things, like one-too-many Jellycats. ## The National Gallery Then: London, National Gallery 1955 - 1955 - Sörmland Museum, Sweden - CC BY-SA ,  Now: View of the National Gallery, Shot with the Lomomatic 110 Camera&Color Tiger 110 ISO 200, May 2026 I am nothing if not an art nerd, so, of course, I had to pay a visit to the National Gallery, a museum with over 2,300 masterpieces in Trafalgar Square. This is one of the spots where I managed to frame the Now & Then photos correctly, and almost everything is the same – give or take a generation and a few columns added. I love the comparison of the dresses of those going about their day. Once inside the museum, it was room after room of stunningly captivating pieces, one of my favorites being The Execution of Lady Jane Grey, painted by Paul Delaroche. ## Notting Hill [](https://www.lomography.com/homes/angelinatacinelli/photos/29466295) [](https://www.lomography.com/homes/angelinatacinelli/photos/29466294) Now: View of Notting Hill ,  Shot with the Lomomatic 110 Camera&Color Tiger 110 ISO 200, May 2026 By the end of the day, we were buzzed on life in London (and maybe something else) and ended up in Notting Hill – an artsy neighborhood known for its pastel-colored homes, and one-a-well-known-rom-com was maybe filmed here. We went right around sunset, and the lighting conditions were a great fit for the 200 ISO. One of my friends took over with shooting the photos, which I feel brought a new perspective and funky feeling to the frames. ## St. James Park [](https://www.lomography.com/homes/angelinatacinelli/photos/29466304) Now: St. James Park ,  Shot with the Lomomatic 110 Camera&Color Tiger 110 ISO 200, May 2026 St. James Park has an interesting history – what started as a leper hospital was transformed by the infamous Henry VIII into a park in 1532. The pelicans (friendly or not) have been living there for almost 350 years. We strolled around the grounds, and I took this photo after seeing a batch of Irises (my favorite flower) growing by the water. The photo I took is my own version of an impressionist painting, en plein air, inspired by what was around me. I remember the wind rustling through the trees as we walked along the paths on our last day in London, and this moment in the park took me out of the bustling city and into a moment of stillness. ## Buckingham Palace Then: Grup Turístic - 1974 by El Centre - Digital Memory of Catalonia, Spain - Public Domain ,  Now: Buckingham Palace, Shot with the Lomomatic 110 Camera&Color Tiger 110 ISO 200, May 2026 Buckingham Palace is probably one of the most recognizable buildings in all of London and serves as the main symbol of the British Monarchy. With 775 rooms, a balcony where everything from wars to weddings has been announced, and a plentiful 78 bathrooms to choose from, it was also a lovely photographic subject. My favorite part about the photos is the Victoria Memorial monument as the golden shades came up beautifully on my film roll. Once again, the framing was not perfect, but the area remains mainly unchanged, aside from the lovely outfits and means of transportation. ## Westminster Abbey Then: "Westminster Abbey, London", c. 1843, Rijksmuseum, P-F-00-2429 ,  Now: Close-Up of Westminster Abbey, Shot with the Lomomatic 110 Camera&Color Tiger 110 ISO 200, May 2026 Westminster Abbey just sounds fancy, and it is. Since 1066, 40 British monarchs have been crowned there and a poet corner honors several old writers that you may have been forced to read in English class. It is right in the center of London and has seen several transformations in its lifetime. The framing of the photo was difficult to get “right” but I feel like the difference in photos are a nod to its dynamic history. I took the photo vertically with my Lomomatic 110 which allowed for more detail in the frame. ## Big Ben Then: London, Westminster Palace, Elizabeth Tower: Uhrturm (auch gen. Big Ben) by Barry, Charles (1795-1860) (Künstler/Hersteller) - Media library of Art and Visual History of Humboldt University of Berlin, Germany - CC BY-SA ,  Now: Big Ben Detail, ,  Shot with the Lomomatic 110 Camera&Color Tiger 110 ISO 200, May 2026 Perhaps my favorite photo from the trip was of Big Ben. I recently learned that the name actually refers to the bell inside, which weighs an extremely light 13.7 tons. The tower is the height of 21 London buses stacked upon each other, and has a wonderfully unique bell ring as it cracked in 1858, giving it its signature sound. Both the photos capture it as it stands tall above the skyline, give or take a lovely lamp pole. This was one of our last stops on our trip, and it seems like we saved the best for last. ## A Classy Cheerio Life never goes according to plan, and neither does film photography. From fumbled frames to imperfections in the development of a film roll, we never know how our photos are going to turn out. I went to London with a plan to recreate some old photos (and eat my weight in Fish and Chips), but the results were not what I expected. At Lomography, we learn to live through and with the chaos. We capture life as it is, and those unforeseen moments in between are the best kind. I encourage you to find what inspires you – whether it is recreating old photos or making new memories of your own. Go out into this world fearlessly. Oh, and don’t forget your film camera, of course.
